Study of CP Violation in Higgs Physics within the 2HDM
Undergraduate Research Program | Advisor: Prof. Yusheng Wu
(Dec 2024 - Dec 2025)
  • Investigated the Type-II Two-Higgs-Doublet Model (2HDM) with soft $Z_2$ symmetry breaking (C2HDM) to explore potential signals of CP violation.
  • Performed comprehensive parameter space scanning using ScannerS, CHDECAY, HiggsBounds and HiggsSignals, applying constraints from direct searches for new physics and the latest measurements of the 125 GeV Higgs boson at the LHC. Developed custom Python scripts to automate systematic parameter scans that fulfill specific research requirements and generating professional visualizations.
  • Analyzed the model's behavior near the Standard Model alignment limit and evaluated the sensitivity of current LHC data to C2HDM parameters via $\chi^2$ statistical analysis.
  • Evaluates the compatibility of Higgs masses and CP violation parameters with current LHC 125 GeV Higgs experiment data, revealing that the SM alignment limit is not strictly the global best-fit point for the CP violation parameters α1 and α2 (where tanβ < 1), and the sensitivities are strictly dependent on tanβ.
  • Future Plan: Implement MC simulations to derive kinematic distributions for calculating STXS bin modification factors; Refine benchmark parameter regions with strict theoretical and experimental constraints to identify distinct CP-violation signals.

High Energy Physics

Course Project: Particle Physics (Graduate) | Personal Study

  • Reviewed definition of Long-Lived Particles (LLPs) in Beyond the Standard Model physics, analyzing mechanisms that suppress decay widths to achieve long lifetimes including small couplings, heavy mediators, and phase space suppression.
  • Surveyed BSM theory categories naturally yielding LLPs such as Supersymmetry-like theories, Higgs-portal theories, Gauge-portal theories, Dark Matter theories and Heavy Neutrino theories, introducing the simplified modelsorganized in terms of LLP channels characterized by a combination of a particular LLP production mode with a particular decay mode.

Inverse Isotope Effect: A Brief Interpretation Based on Anharmonic Effects in Lattice Vibrations and Electron-Phonon Coupling (Jan 2026)

Course Project: Solid State Physics A | Personal Study

  • Investigated the anomalous inverse isotope effect in palladium hydrides (PdHx), where the superconducting critical temperature (Tc) exhibits an unexpected dependence on isotopic mass that contradicts the BCS theory prediction of M-0.5 scaling.
  • Derived the electron-phonon coupling constant (λ) from the Fröhlich Hamiltonian, using the second quantization formalism and the second-order perturbation theory.
  • Constructed a one-dimensional anharmonic toy model to demonstrate how large zero-point motion induces significant anharmonic corrections to the effective force constant and substantially modifies the electron-phonon coupling behavior.
  • Applied the McMillan equation to show that strong anharmonicity can reverse the conventional mass dependence of Tc, providing a qualitative theoretical explanation for the inverse isotope effect in PdHx.
  • Project Report: Download PDF
